A wedding invitation sets the dress code. Most guests ignore it. That's how you end up in a cotton sundress at a black-tie ballroom or a velvet gown at a backyard ceremony. The dress code isn't a suggestion — it's the cue for floor length, fabric weight, accessories, and shoes. Decoded once, the rest of the season becomes easier to shop.

## Read the dress code first

Before you do anything, look at the invitation twice. The wording tells you four things: formality, season, venue, and time of day. "Black-tie optional" means floor length. "Garden attire" means midi or shorter, plus a shoe you can stand on grass in. "Cocktail attire" is the most flexible and the most often misinterpreted — it means knee-to-midi length and a refined fabric, never a club dress.

Time matters. Evening weddings lean darker and more formal. Afternoon weddings lean lighter, softer, and more relaxed. Beach and resort weddings lean toward breathable fabric and flat shoes, regardless of what the dress code says.

When the dress code is missing entirely, default to "formal" or "cocktail." Showing up too dressed is rarely a problem. Showing up underdressed is.

## White-tie weddings: floor-length gowns, gloves optional

White-tie is rare outside state dinners and ultra-traditional ceremonies. The dress is floor-length and structured: silk, satin, crepe, or taffeta. No prints, no jersey, no casual fabrics.

A $695 Self-Portrait floral jacquard gown or a $1,295 Galvan satin column from Net-a-Porter does the job without looking costume-like. BHLDN carries wedding-adjacent formal gowns (the Joanna August line, $298–$598) that read white-tie-adjacent at garden-formal settings.

Avoid: anything strapless without structure, anything jersey, anything cocktail-length. White-tie also expects a closed-toe heel — $695 Jimmy Choo or $375 Sam Edelman pumps work.

## Black-tie and black-tie optional: the floor-length tier

Black-tie is the most common formal dress code. Floor length gowns, refined fabrics, and either covered shoulders or a respectful neckline. Black-tie optional simply means floor length or a tailored jumpsuit.

Solid colors photograph well — emerald, sapphire, deep burgundy, ink navy, classic black. A $248 Aritzia satin slip dress, a $295 Reformation gown in silk crepe, a $488 Mac Duggal trumpet gown, a $995 Self-Portrait lace gown are all reliable picks.

Accessories: heel $425 Christian Louboutin or $145 Schutz; clutch $175–$295 from Clare V. or Cult Gaia; jewelry small and intentional — gold or pearl studs plus one piece.

For black-tie optional, a $218 Aritzia tailored jumpsuit in crepe or wool reads as a gown without the gown.

## Formal and semi-formal: the cocktail tier

Most weddings land here. Formal leans toward midi or floor-length; semi-formal says knee-to-midi, refined fabrics.

A formal wedding: a $295 Reformation "Brigitte" midi dress, a $428 Self-Portrait floral midi, a $398 Aritzia satin midi. Semi-formal: a $168 Aritzia contour dress, a $188 Reformation "Liat" mini, a $238 BHLDN crepe midi.

Covering the shoulders is recommended for any religious venue. A $98 COS cashmere cardigan or a $145 Aritzia cropped blazer over a sleeveless dress handles this without looking like you're heading to brunch.

## Cocktail attire: the most flexible tier

Cocktail means midi or just above the knee, refined fabric (silk, crepe, satin, jacquard), and a heel or dressed-up flat. Color and print both work.

Reformation's cocktail line ($195–$345) is built for this. Aritzia's Wilfred line ($118–$248) carries contemporary cuts. Revolve carries more than 200 cocktail dresses under $300. Net-a-Porter's editor picks lean higher-end ($450+).

Jumpsuits belong here. A $148 Aritzia tailored crepe jumpsuit or a $168 Theory jumpsuit reads cocktail and skips the dress entirely.

## Garden and outdoor weddings: dress for grass and weather

Garden weddings expect midi to mid-calf, breathable fabric, and shoes with a block heel or a flat. Avoid stilettos that sink into grass. Avoid floor-length gowns that drag through wet grass.

A $245 Reformation linen midi, a $188 Doen floral dress, a $148 Aritzia botanical-print midi all suit a garden setting. Bring a light layer — a $98 COS wool-cashmere cardigan or a $185 cropped trench from Aritzia handles cool evening air.

Avoid white, ivory, cream, and pastel pink (close to blush). Avoid heavy fabrics like velvet and satin. Slip and chiffon work, but only if the dress has a slip underneath.

## Beach weddings: barefoot-tolerance matters

Beach weddings expect short to midi, breathable fabric, and shoes that handle sand. A wedge ankle strap ($145 at Schutz, $185 at Sam Edelman) or a flat leather sandal ($185 at Aritzia, $215 at Loeffler Randall) handles soft ground.

A $118 Doen cotton midi, a $148 Aritzia linen wrap dress, a $215 Faithful the Brand floral midi are reliable picks. Prints work well against a beach backdrop — leaves, watercolor florals, tile patterns.

White, off-white, and cream are generally avoided, but a $1,800 dress with strong color blocking or a print that includes white reads as a patterned look. Solid white reads as the bride.

## Casual weddings: dressed-up daywear, not Sunday clothes

Casual weddings are the most misunderstood. "Casual" doesn't mean jeans. It means dressed-up daywear: a simple dress, a skirt-and-top combination, tailored trousers with a silk blouse. Tasteful, but not formal.

A $68 Everlane cotton midi, a $98 Aritzia bias-cut slip dress, a $78 Madewell floral mini with $98 Everlane flat sandals. A $148 COS linen jumpsuit with leather sneakers also works.

Avoid: clubwear, anything clubwear-adjacent, gym fabric, athletic wear.

## Country, barn, and rustic weddings: texture matters

A country or barn venue leans natural. Linen, cotton, light wool, denim. Avoid anything that looks like a city ballroom dress — satin and high gloss don't suit a hayloft.

A $188 Doen floral midi, a $225 Ralph Lauren denim shirtdress, a $148 COS linen jumpsuit, a $245 Reformation cotton midi all handle the brief. Footwear needs a block heel or a flat boot — a $148 Doc Martens Chelsea or a $178 Aritzia knee-high boot handles a gravel path.

Toss a $145 Aritzia cropped denim jacket over a dress for cool evening air.

## Accessories, makeup, and hair by dress code

Dress codes cascade through accessories. Black-tie expects fine jewelry (pearls, diamonds, or clean gold) and an evening clutch. Semi-formal allows statement earrings or a single bold piece. Garden and casual expect smaller jewelry and a structured handbag or tote.

Heels follow the same pattern: black-tie expects a stiletto or slim block heel; semi-formal allows anything from stiletto to chunky block; garden and casual expect a block heel or a flat.

Hair: a low bun or a soft wave for black-tie and formal; a half-up style or loose waves for semi-formal and cocktail; a relaxed bun for beach and garden. Avoid stiff curls that look like prom.

Makeup: black-tie allows smoky eye, deeper lip, more coverage. Cocktail and semi-formal leans neutral with one feature focus. Garden and casual lean dewy, lighter, and more skin-forward.

## What not to wear to any wedding

A few universal rules.

Don't wear white, ivory, cream, champagne, blush, or pale pink. The bride is the only one in those tones.

Don't wear anything that photographs as a costume. Sequins can work in moderation; full-sequin gowns read as a different event.

Don't wear anything too revealing for the venue — a mini dress at a synagogue or a strapless gown at a Catholic church reads wrong.

Don't upstage the bride. Anything that draws the room's attention past the ceremony isn't working.

Don't wear heavy perfume. The couple will be in close quarters all evening.

Don't ignore the dress code. Showing up in a cocktail dress when the invitation says black-tie is the most common guest complaint.

## The bottom line

Read the dress code on the invitation twice, choose a midi or floor-length dress from a refined fabric in a color that won't echo the bride, and pick one statement accessory. Everything else follows.
