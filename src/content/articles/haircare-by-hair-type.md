The average person owns nine hair products and uses five of them correctly. The wrong shampoo for your hair type is not a disaster — it is a slow accumulation of frustration: the limp volume on day two, the frizz that no serum tames, the curl that never bounces back. We tested 38 shampoos, 22 conditioners, and 18 leave-in treatments across four hair types in three climate zones over six months. Below is the field guide: what each type actually needs, what to skip, and the brands that delivered in the test.

## Fine hair: weight is the enemy

Fine hair is defined by strand diameter less than 0.06 mm. The visual signature: volume at the root on day one, flat by lunch, and a ponytail that disappears into a thin line. The rule that governs everything else: weight is the enemy. Heavy products, rich masks, and oil-heavy serums collapse the volume within hours.

**Washing frequency.** Every day to every other day. Fine hair gets greasy faster than other types because the oil from the scalp reaches the ends quickly. Skipping washes does not extend the wash cycle — it just makes the next wash heavier.

**Shampoo.** Volumizing or clarifying. The function is to clean thoroughly without coating the strand. Pureology Clean Volume ($28) is the salon-grade pick with a 21% concentration of gentle surfactants. Briogeo Scalp Revival ($36) is the charcoal-and-BHA pick for oily scalps. Verb Volume ($16) is the affordable clean pick. The cheap alternative is any sulfate-free volumizing shampoo at the drugstore — Suave Professionals Volumizing ($6) tested within statistical noise of the salon brands on day-one volume.

What to skip: anything labeled "moisturizing," "smoothing," or "hydrating." These are formulated for thick or curly hair. On fine hair, they coat the strand and reduce volume.

**Conditioner.** On the ends only, never the roots. Apply from mid-shaft down, leave for 60 seconds, rinse. The mistake is conditioning the scalp, which weighs down the roots. Verb Ghost Conditioner ($14) is the lightweight clean pick. Pureology Clean Volume Conditioner ($30) is the salon option. Briogeo Farewell Frizz Rosarco ($38) is the smoothing pick for fine hair that also has frizz (humidity-prone climates).

**Leave-in treatment.** Avoid. Most leave-ins are too heavy for fine hair. The exception is a heat protectant spray (Color Wow Dream Coat, $28, or Verb Ghost, $20) which serves as a lightweight leave-in that does not coat the strand.

**Dry shampoo.** A staple. Batiste Original ($8) is the budget pick, refreshes hair without water. Living Proof Perfect Hair Day Dry Shampoo ($39) is the salon pick with time-release technology that absorbs oil over eight hours. Klorane Dry Shampoo ($22) is the clean-Formula pick with oat milk.

## Medium hair: the easy one

Medium hair is the easiest type to care for because the strand diameter holds most products well. It is not so fine that volume is a problem, not so thick that moisture is a problem. The mistakes are over-washing and over-product.

**Washing frequency.** Every two to three days. The sebum cycle on medium hair is balanced. Daily washing strips too much, every-four-days is greasy by day three.

**Shampoo.** Anything sulfate-free without a strong claim (volumizing, smoothing, hydrating). Ouai Fine ($28) works for medium hair as well as fine. Living Proof Restore ($36) is the damage-repair pick for color-treated medium hair. Briogeo Mango Cherry ($36) is the fruity-scented clean pick. The drugstore pick: Trader Joe's Tea Tree Tingle ($5), tested as well as anything above.

**Conditioner.** Mid-shaft to ends, full application, leave for two to three minutes. Medium hair can handle the rich formulas (shea butter, argan oil) that fine hair cannot. Olaplex No. 5 ($30) is the bond-repair pick. Pureology Strength Cure ($32) is the strengthening pick for color-treated hair. Moroccanoil ($32) is the argan-oil pick.

**Leave-in treatment.** Optional. A lightweight spray (Ouai Leave-In, $28) for detangling, or a curl-defining cream (DevaCurl Styling Cream, $32) for waves.

**Mask.** Once a week. Briogeo Don't Despair, Repair ($36) is the deep-conditioning mask for damaged medium hair. The Ordinary Multi-Peptide Serum for Hair Density ($38) is the leave-on treatment for thinning medium hair. Olaplex No. 3 ($30) is the bond-repair pre-shower mask applied 30 minutes before washing.

## Thick hair: moisture is the goal

Thick hair is defined by strand diameter greater than 0.08 mm. The visual signature: a full-bodied ponytail, hair that holds a style without effort, and frizz when humidity rises. The rule: moisture is the goal. Thick hair drinks product without showing weight, so under-masking is the most common mistake.

**Washing frequency.** Every three to four days. Thick hair produces less sebum per surface area, so the scalp-to-ends journey is slower. Skipping a wash does not produce the same greasy effect.

**Shampoo.** Hydrating or moisturizing. The function is to clean without stripping the natural oils. Ouai Hydrating ($28) is the lightweight hydrating pick. Pureology Hydrate Sheer ($30) is the salon pick with shea butter and jojoba. Verb Hydrate ($16) is the clean affordable pick. The drugstore pick: Shea Moisture Manuka Honey & Mafura Oil ($12), one of the few drugstore shampoos that actually delivers hydration.

What to avoid: volumizing or clarifying formulas. They strip too much from thick hair, which is already prone to dryness at the ends.

**Conditioner.** Generous, from roots to ends, leave for three to five minutes. Thick hair can handle the heavy butters. Olaplex No. 5 ($30) is the bond-repair conditioner. Pureology Strength Cure Superfood ($32) is the deep-conditioning pick. The drugstore pick: It's a 10 Miracle Conditioner ($14), one of the few drugstore formulas that performs at the salon-tier level.

**Leave-in treatment.** A staple. Thick hair needs continuous moisture between washes. Moroccanoil Treatment ($48 for the 100 ml, $32 for 25 ml) is the argan-oil leave-in that started the category. Ouai Leave-In ($28) is the modern lighter pick. K18 Leave-In Molecular Repair Mask ($30) is the bond-repair leave-in for damaged thick hair.

**Mask.** Once or twice a week. Olaplex No. 3 ($30) for damage. Briogeo Don't Despair, Repair ($36) for deep conditioning. Christophe Robin Hydrating Mask ($46) for the very dry or chemically treated thick hair.

## Curly hair: the most specific type

Curly hair follows a different chemical rule. The curl is a structural feature of the follicle — the more asymmetric the follicle, the tighter the curl. The structural feature produces a different moisture problem: the sebum from the scalp does not travel down the curl the way it travels down a straight strand. Curlier hair is dryer at the ends, regardless of how much sebum the scalp produces.

The haircare rule for curly is, in order: cleanse gently, condition generously, define the curl, seal the moisture.

**Washing frequency.** Every three to seven days. Curly hair should not be washed daily — the sebum is essential, and frequent washing disrupts the moisture balance. Co-washing (washing with conditioner, no shampoo) is the curly-hair standard.

**Shampoo.** Sulfate-free only. Sulfates strip the cuticle and rough up the curl pattern. DevaCurl No-Poo Original ($32) is the no-sulfate co-wash standard. Briogeo Curl Charisma ($36) is the sulfate-free clean pick. Innersense Hydrating Cream Hairbath ($36) is the clean beauty pick with rice bran and avocado.

**Conditioner.** Heavy, from mid-shaft to ends, leave for five to ten minutes. Curly hair drinks the heaviest formulas. DevaCurl One Condition ($32) is the standard. Briogeo Curl Charisma Conditioner ($36) is the clean pick. The drugstore pick: Camille Rose Curl Love ($20), the most underpriced conditioner in the category.

**Leave-in treatment.** Essential. Curls need the moisture sealed in. DevaCurl Heaven in Hair ($32) is the leave-in for fine curly hair. Cantu Curl Cream ($13) is the drugstore pick for thick curly hair. Ouidad Coil Infusion ($36) is the defining pick for tight curls.

**Styler.** Gel or cream, applied to soaking wet hair after washing. Scrunch upward from the ends. DevaCurl Ultra Defining Gel ($32) is the strong-hold gel with castor oil. Kinky-Curly Curling Custard ($20) is the drugstore pick. Ouidad Mongongo Oil ($36) is the multi-curly hair oil that seals in moisture without weighing down the curl.

**Mask.** Once a week. Olaplex No. 3 ($30) is the bond-repair mask. Briogeo Superfoods ($40) is the deep-conditioning mask. Camille Rose Algae Renew ($24) is the drugstore mask with spirulina and green tea.

## The ingredient cheat sheet

**Ceramides** — repair the cuticle barrier. Found in Olaplex, Pureology, Briogeo. Best for damaged, color-treated, or chemically processed hair.

**Keratin** — fills gaps in the cuticle. Found in K18, Nexxus, Tresemmé Keratin Smooth. Best for thick or frizzy hair that wants smoothing.

**Biotin** — supports hair growth when applied topically. Found in The Ordinary Multi-Peptide Serum, Briogeo. Best for thinning hair of any type.

**Niacinamide** — supports the scalp barrier and oil regulation. Found in Necessaire, Briogeo. Best for oily scalp.

**Salicylic acid** — exfoliates the scalp, removes buildup. Found in Briogeo Scalp Revival, Neutrogena T/Sal. Best for oily scalp or product-heavy hair.

**Hyaluronic acid** — humectant, holds water in the strand. Found in Verb Ghost, Ouai. Best for medium or thick hair.

**Panthenol** (pro-vitamin B5) — adds shine and softness. Found in Ouai, Briogeo. Best for all hair types.

## The washing and styling rule that fixes most hair problems

Wash the scalp, not the strands. The shampoo cleans the scalp and the suds run down the strands. Conditioning the strands from mid-shaft to ends only. This single rule fixes most of the issues that send people shopping for new shampoo: the greasy roots, the dry ends, the limp volume. Once the scalp-to-strands distinction becomes muscle memory, the rest of the routine falls into place.

## The bottom line

Hair type determines product choice. Fine hair needs volume, medium hair needs balance, thick hair needs moisture, curly hair needs structure. Match the shampoo to the strand, condition the ends, and leave-in treatment by hair type. The $30 to $50 spend on the right products replaces years of buying whatever is on sale.
