A robot vacuum used to mean a bumper car that bounced off walls until the battery died. The 2024 generation maps your floor, dodges pet droppings, empties its own bin, and refills its own mop water. The bad ones still bounce around aimlessly; the good ones run while you're at work and leave behind floors you can walk barefoot across without picking up a sock.

The harder question is which one deserves your floor plan. A $200 robot with no mapping will frustrate you within a week. A $1,500 flagship with full automation will save you a few hours a month — if the mapping, suction, and mopping all hold up. Specs don't tell the whole story: a 6,000 Pa robot with bad navigation is worse in practice than a 3,000 Pa robot that finds every room.

## Navigation: LiDAR, camera, vSLAM, and bumper-car

How a robot finds its way around your home is the single biggest spec to get right. There are four common systems and they produce wildly different results.

**LiDAR** (Roborock, Dreame, Ecovacs, most premium brands) uses a spinning laser to map the room in real time. LiDAR works in the dark, produces centimeter-accurate maps, and is the fastest at cleaning a defined area. The Roborock S8 Pro Ultra ($1,599), Dreame L20 Ultra ($1,499), and Ecovacs Deebot X2 Omni ($1,299) all use LiDAR. LiDAR is the gold standard — get a LiDAR robot if the budget stretches.

**Camera-based navigation** (iRobot Roomba j7/s9, Switchbot K10+) uses an upward-facing camera and visual landmarks to localize itself. It's nearly as accurate as LiDAR in well-lit rooms but struggles in the dark. Some cameras also feed into AI obstacle detection — iRobot's Roomba j7+ uses a front-facing camera to identify pet waste, shoes, and cords and avoid them. Camera navigation is good; LiDAR is better.

**vSLAM (visual SLAM)** (older iRobot models, some Eufy robots) uses a low-resolution camera with computer vision. It's cheaper than LiDAR and works in any light, but it's noticeably less accurate. Some vSLAM robots lose their position when light changes suddenly (someone opens a curtain mid-run).

**Bumper-car / gyroscope** (budget Eufy, Roborock, and Ilife models under $300) drives in a pattern until it hits something, then turns. These robots don't build a map, don't clean in straight lines, and miss big patches on every run. They work for a small apartment with one room — they're not a real cleaning tool for any larger space.

If you have a multi-room home, pay the premium for LiDAR or premium camera navigation. The difference is between a robot you trust to run unsupervised and one you have to baby.

## Suction: Pa, brush rolls, and what actually lifts pet hair

Suction is measured in pascals (Pa). Roborock and Dreame now publish 6,000-18,000 Pa ratings — the numbers have climbed dramatically in the last two years. iRobot doesn't publish Pa at all and prefers to talk about "10x the suction" relative to older models.

The honest truth: anything above 3,000 Pa is enough for daily debris on hard floors and low-pile carpet. High-pile carpet and pet hair on rugs benefit from 5,000 Pa or more. Above 8,000 Pa, the robot pulls harder but rarely cleans meaningfully better; the limiting factor becomes the brush roll and the bin, not the motor.

Brush design matters more than peak suction. Rubber/silicone brush rolls (iRobot Roomba s9, Roborock S8) don't tangle with hair the way bristle brushes do. If you have long hair or shedding pets, a rubber brush rolls saves you 10 minutes a month of cutting hair off the roller.

Most premium robots also auto-raise the mop pad on carpet (Roborock S8, Dreame L20, Ecovacs X2) so a wet mop doesn't touch your rug. Look for this if you have mixed flooring.

## Mopping: pads, sonic vibration, and mop-lifting bases

The mopping function has improved faster than any other feature in the category. Five tiers in active use:

- **Basic drag mop.** A damp microfiber pad is dragged across the floor. Mostly useless — it smears wet messes around and leaves streaks. Don't pay extra for this. Most sub-$300 robots have it.
- **Vibrating mop.** A pad that scrubs at 3,000-4,000 vibrations per minute. Roborock S7/S8, Dreame L10, and Eufy X10 all use vibrating mops. Removes dried spills in a single pass.
- **Sonic mop.** Pad vibrates at 10,000+ times per minute. Roborock S8 Pro Ultra uses this. Cleans sticky messes better than vibrating.
- **Spinning twin pads.** Two round mop pads rotate in opposite directions. Ecovacs Deebot X2 Omni, Dreame L20 Ultra use this. Best stain removal in the category.
- **Mop-lifting base.** The robot returns to the dock, the pads are washed with clean water, and dirty water is pumped into a tank in the base. Self-cleaning mops are the single biggest quality-of-life upgrade. Roborock S8 Pro Ultra, Dreame L20 Ultra, Ecovacs X2 Omni all do this.

A clean water tank in the base is 2.5-3 liters and runs the robot through 4-6 mop cycles before needing a refill. Some bases also auto-dispense cleaning solution — a small convenience that keeps the mop pads from smelling sour.

## Self-emptying docks: the upgrade that earns its $300

Most robot vacuums under $500 ship with a tiny onboard dustbin (300-500 mL) that needs to be emptied manually after every run. A self-emptying dock solves this: the robot docks, the dock sucks the bin out through a port, and the debris goes into a disposable bag in the dock.

Dock bag sizes range from 2.5L (iRobot j7+, Roborock Q8 Max+) to 3L (Dreame L20, Ecovacs X2) to 4L (Roborock S8 Pro Ultra). At normal use (every-other-day vacuuming), a 2.5L bag lasts 4-6 weeks. A 4L bag lasts 6-10 weeks. Bags cost $25-50 for a pack of 6.

Self-cleaning docks also have a clean water tank, a dirty water tank, a solution reservoir, and — in the most premium models — a hot-water mop wash that kills bacteria between runs. The Roborock S8 Pro Ultra, Dreame L20 Ultra, and Ecovacs X2 Omni all have hot-water mop washing.

If you have pets or a large home, a self-emptying dock is the single best upgrade. It reduces robot vacuum maintenance from weekly to monthly.

## Obstacle avoidance: pet poop, cables, and the mess that ruins a robot

The most expensive failure mode is the robot running over a pet accident and smearing it across the whole floor. iRobot's "Pet Owner Official Promise" (POOP) — a free replacement guarantee if the j7+ runs over pet waste — exists because this happens.

Modern obstacle avoidance uses a combination of cameras, structured light, and AI to identify objects on the floor. iRobot Roomba j7+ uses a front-facing camera with on-device AI trained specifically to identify pet waste. Roborock, Dreame, and Ecovacs use cameras plus 3D structured light for general obstacle recognition — good with shoes, cables, and furniture legs.

If you have pets, iRobot's obstacle avoidance is currently the most reliable at pet waste. If you don't, any of the premium LiDAR robots will avoid cables and small objects well enough.

## App and mapping: zones, no-go lines, multi-floor

Every premium robot comes with an app — Roborock, iRobot, Ecovacs, Eufy, Dreame, Switchbot all have their own. The differences come down to how good the map editor is.

Features that matter:

- **Room labeling.** The robot maps your home; you name the rooms. "Clean kitchen" works after this.
- **No-go zones and virtual walls.** Draw a box around the dog's water bowl and the robot won't enter.
- **Multi-floor maps.** Save maps for multiple floors; the robot picks the right one on each run.
- **Zone cleaning.** "Clean under the kitchen table" works as a single command.
- **Carpet detection.** Auto-boost suction on rugs, auto-lift mops on carpet.

Roborock and Ecovacs apps have the best map editors. iRobot's app is OK but less customizable. Switchbot's app is basic. Dreame's app is functional but slower than Roborock's.

## The brand matrix: who makes what

**iRobot Roomba** is the original and still the reliability leader. The j7+ ($599) is the best pet-aware robot — obstacle avoidance, self-emptying, smart mapping. The s9+ ($999) is the flagship with a D-shape that cleans edges and corners better than round robots. The Combo j5+ ($649) is the vacuum/mop hybrid. iRobot doesn't publish Pa but the actual cleaning is excellent — especially on pet hair.

**Roborock** is the value flagship pick. The Q8 Max+ ($549) is a LiDAR self-emptying robot with mopping — best budget choice in the line. The S8 ($749) adds sonic mopping and rubber brushes. The S8 Pro Ultra ($1,599) is the flagship with hot-water mop washing, self-emptying, and refill. Roborock's app is the best in the category.

**Dreame** is the suction and mopping specialist. The L10s Ultra ($1,099) is a LiDAR self-emptying robot with twin spinning mop pads. The L20 Ultra ($1,499) adds mop extension to reach edges and baseboards. The X30 Ultra ($1,899) adds hot-water mop washing and AI camera obstacle avoidance. Dreame ships the highest Pa numbers in the category.

**Ecovacs Deebot** is the feature-packed alternative. The N10 ($400) is the budget LiDAR pick. The T20 Omni ($899) is a self-emptying mop-lifting robot with hot-water washing. The X2 Omni ($1,299) has a square shape for better edge cleaning. Ecovacs packs more features per dollar than anyone.

**Eufy** is the entry-midrange brand. The 11S MAX ($230) is the cheapest robot worth buying. The X10 Pro Omni ($750) is the midrange flagship with LiDAR, mopping, and self-emptying. Eufy robots are quieter than most competitors but the navigation is a step behind Roborock.

**Switchbot** is the small-home specialist. The K10+ ($430) is a 9.7" diameter robot — the smallest in the category, fits under furniture other robots can't reach. The S1 ($700) is the mopping flagship. Switchbot robots work best in apartments and small rooms.

**Neato** is the older alternative. The D9 ($400) is a LiDAR robot with a D-shape. Neato's software has been less updated than the category leaders but the hardware is solid.

## How to pick the right robot for you

- **Apartment under 800 sq ft, mostly hard floor:** Eufy 11S MAX ($230) or Switchbot K10+ ($430) for tight spaces.
- **House 1,500-2,500 sq ft, mixed flooring, want it done right:** Roborock Q8 Max+ ($549) or Eufy X10 Pro Omni ($750).
- **Multi-pet household, fear the pet poop problem:** iRobot Roomba j7+ ($599) — buy for the POOP guarantee.
- **Mixed flooring, want best mopping in the category:** Roborock S8 Pro Ultra ($1,599) — mop washing, self-emptying, sonic mopping, rubber brushes.
- **Tight budget, want LiDAR:** Ecovacs N10 ($400) — no frills but the mapping works.
- **Smart home integration first:** Roborock — supports Alexa, Google, Siri Shortcuts, and Matter.

## The bottom line

A LiDAR robot with a self-emptying dock and a mop-lifting base — like the Roborock Q8 Max+ ($549) at the budget end or the S8 Pro Ultra ($1,599) at the flagship end — covers 90% of homes for between $550 and $1,600. Skip the sub-$300 robots unless your floor plan is a single room, and pay for a self-emptying dock if pets or a busy household mean the bin fills faster than you want to empty it.
