Most professional wardrobes are not actually small — they are unfocused. People own 12 black tops and no trousers. They own six blazers and three of them are the same shade. The closet looks full. Getting dressed still takes 20 minutes and produces nothing to wear.

We have built workwear wardrobes for ourselves, for friends, for a couple of clients. The same 12–15 pieces show up every time. Below is the inventory, the logic of which pieces do what, and the color system that lets them all mix. The brands are real: Aritzia, Theory, COS, Cuyana, Everlane, Madewell, Toteme. None paid.

## The philosophy: separates over suits

The single biggest mistake in workwear is buying a full matching suit. You wear it twice a week, the trousers get worn through faster than the jacket, and you have nowhere to wear the jacket alone. Suits are for weddings, depositions, and pitches where everyone else is wearing one.

A workwear wardrobe built on **separates** is more useful. A blazer that goes with three trousers. A trouser that goes with four tops. A cardigan that works with everything. You own fewer pieces and wear each of them more often. This is the math behind every capsule wardrobe that actually works.

Build the wardrobe around:
- 2 blazers (one neutral, one textural)
- 2 trouser silhouettes (one straight, one pleated or wide)
- 5 tops in compatible colors
- 2 knits (one cardigan, one crewneck)
- 1 dress
- 1 coat
- 2 bags
- 1–2 pairs of shoes

That is roughly 15 pieces. From those 15, you can build a week's worth of outfits without repeating a look.

## The 15 pieces, in concrete form

**1. The neutral blazer — $245 COS Wool Blazer or $395 Theory Wool Blazer**

Single-breasted, one-button, in a year-round weight wool. Charcoal, navy, or stone. This is the blazer you wear to every meeting that is not a final round. It should fit at the shoulders (the only non-negotiable fit point) and skim the hip.

**2. The textural blazer — $178 Everlane Italian Wool Blazer or $220 Aritzia Wilfred Vintage Blazer**

A second blazer in a different fabric — houndstooth, glen plaid, corduroy, or a soft cream wool. This is the one that signals you care about clothes without trying too hard. Pair it with jeans on Friday.

**3. The classic trouser — $148 Aritzia Effortless Pant or $178 COS Wool Trousers**

Wide or straight leg, high-rise, in a wool or wool-blend. Black, charcoal, or camel. The most worn item in the wardrobe. Pair with the blazer for meetings, with the cardigan on casual days.

**4. The pleated trouser — $148 Everlane Italian Wool Pleated Trouser or $222 Frame Le High Pleated**

Tailored with a pressed crease and a slightly tapered leg. Adds shape under longer tops. Khaki, navy, or dark brown for separates variety.

**5. The straight-cut jean — $98 Levi's 501 or $148 Madewell Perfect Vintage**

A dark, well-fitting jean with no distressing. Works for casual office days, meetings where you want to look relaxed, and after-hours. The one jean you keep unwrinkled in the rotation.

**6. The white button-down — $98 Everlane Poplin or $148 Aritzia Babaton Poplin**

Crisp, fitted but not tight, in a poplin or broadcloth. White is more versatile than light blue. The most worn top in any wardrobe. Tuck into the trousers, layer under the blazer, or wear alone.

**7. The striped or patterned button-down — $148 Madewell Courier Shirt or $178 Aritzia Barrymore**

A second button-down in a stripe or a small check. Adds visual variety without committing to color. Goes under the blazer the same way the white one does.

**8. The silk shell — $148 Cuyana Classic Shell or $178 Aritzia Wilfred Cami**

In a matte satin or crepe, not shiny. Ivory, black, or olive. Worn alone under the blazer, layered under a cardigan, or tucked into the pleated trouser.

**9. The fine-gauge crewneck — $148 COS Wool Crewneck or $178 Aritzia New Wave Crewneck**

A lightweight merino or cashmere sweater, fitted but not tight. Black, navy, or oatmeal. Layers over the shell, under the blazer, or alone.

**10. The cardigan — $158 Aritzia Effortless Cardigan or $148 COS Cashmere Cardigan**

Mid-weight, falls at the hip or slightly below. The most versatile layering piece in the wardrobe. Wear over a button-down, with the wide-leg trouser, or as outerwear in mild weather.

**11. The black dress — $148 Aritzia Sculpted Knit Dress or $178 COS Knit Dress**

A simple, well-fitted, knee-length dress in a knit or structured crepe. Black is easiest. Wear under the blazer for client meetings, alone for dinners, with the long coat on cold days.

**12. The trench or topcoat — $245 COS Wool Trench or $450 Toteme T-Lock**

Mid-weight, knee-length, in a neutral. The single outerwear piece you wear 200 days a year. Beige, stone, navy, or black.

**13. The work bag — $295 Cuyana Classic Easy Tote or $295 Dagne Dover Allyn**

Structured, fits a 13" laptop, in leather or a quality vegan leather. Black, chestnut, or stone. Not brown — brown bags limit your outfit combinations more than they help.

**14. The second bag — $178 Aritzia Slouchy Bag or $148 Cuyana Mini Structured Tote**

A smaller, more casual bag for weekends, travel, and casual days. Different proportion than the work bag — that is the point.

**15. The shoes — $245 Aeyde Emerald pumps (or any $200 leather loafer) and $185 Everlane Day Gloves**

Closed-toe, leather, in a heel height you can stand in for eight hours. Black or dark brown pumps plus a flat or loafer covers everything. If you wear sneakers at work, replace the second shoe with a $120 leather sneaker from Koio or Common Projects.

## The color palette that makes it work

This is the hidden system behind every cohesive workwear wardrobe. All 15 pieces pull from the same five colors, which means any top goes with any bottom.

The default palette we recommend:
- **Black** (anchor, ground)
- **Charcoal or navy** (depth, contrast)
- **Camel or stone** (warmth, softness)
- **White or ivory** (freshness, foundation)
- **Olive or oxblood** (the accent — one piece only)

That is five colors. Every piece you buy falls into one of them. The blazer is charcoal. The trouser is black. The cardigan is camel. The shell is ivory. The dress is black. The accent — a burgundy knit, an olive pleated trouser — adds the only color that breaks the neutral base.

This is why capsule wardrobe advice keeps failing for people: they buy neutrals but include six shades of beige. Three colors in five pieces is a wardrobe. Six colors in twelve pieces is chaos.

The one piece that breaks the palette — the burgundy knit, the olive trouser — is the seasonal purchase that makes the wardrobe feel current. Most years, that one new piece is enough. If you are tempted to buy a fourth neutral, buy the accent instead.

## Suit versus blazer versus separates

A note on terminology, because this confuses people.

A **suit** is a matching jacket and trouser in the same fabric. You buy it as a pair, you wear it as a pair, and the components do not mix with much else. Suits are for events and dress codes that demand them.

A **blazer** is a jacket (often in a solid color, sometimes patterned) that is meant to mix. You pair a navy blazer with khaki trousers, gray wool, or jeans. A blazer has more versatility than a suit jacket because it was designed for it.

**Separates** are individual pieces — trousers, blazers, tops, knits — that mix and match. The workwear wardrobe above is built from separates.

If you only own one jacket, make it a blazer, not a suit jacket. A blazer looks correct with trousers and with jeans. A suit jacket looks orphaned the moment you wear it with anything else.

## Care and maintenance

A wardrobe of 15 well-cared-for pieces lasts longer than a closet of 50 neglected ones.

- **Wool and cashmere**: brush weekly with a horsehair brush. Dry clean once per season, not monthly. Steam between wears instead of washing.
- **Trousers**: press the creases after every third wear. Hang by the hem on clip hangers to preserve the press.
- **Silk**: hand wash or dry clean. Never machine dry. Steam, don't iron.
- **Leather bags**: condition every 6 months with a leather balm. Store stuffed with tissue to keep shape.
- **Leather shoes**: condition monthly. Use shoe trees (cedar, not plastic) every night. Resole before the heel wears through to the upper.

## The bottom line

Build the wardrobe from 15 separates in a five-color palette, with two blazers, two trouser silhouettes, and one good pair of dark jeans. Spend more on fewer pieces ($150–$250 each, not $50 on twenty). The blazer is the most important jacket to own — not the matching suit.
